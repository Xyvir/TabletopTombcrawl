	\begin{multicols}{3}
		
		\rh{Objective \& scoring}
		Build your hero, explore the tomb, and survive long enough to slay the \kw{Boss} or empty all eight
		hallways (\kw{Clear Tomb}). When either goal is met—or whenever you choose to flee—take the \kw{exit}
		to end the run and bank your \kw{gold}:
		\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=8pt,itemsep=0.15pt,topsep=0.3pt,parsep=0pt,label=\suit{$\bullet$}]
			\item \kw{1 gold} per \kw{Rat} (Ace--Queen of the three non-boss suits; 36 cards).
			\item \kw{2 gold} per \kw{Monster} (Ace--Queen of the Boss's suit; 12 cards).
			\item \kw{5 gold} per \kw{General} (the three non-boss Kings).
			\item \kw{25 gold} for the \kw{Boss} (100 gold total for a full clear).
		\end{itemize}
		
		\rh{Setup}
		Shuffle a standard 52-card deck and deal as follows:
		\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=10pt,itemsep=0.15pt,topsep=0.3pt,parsep=0pt]
			\item \kw{The Hero:} Deal the top four cards face up into the four left-most pockets of the \kw{Status
				Track}, in order: \kw{Kin}, \kw{Class}, \kw{Glyph}, and \kw{Item}.
			\item \kw{The Hero Chip:} Place the \kw{Hero Chip} on the divider immediately right of the Item pocket,
			separating your four hero aspects from the three plain pockets (a Dwarf unlocks an eighth).
			\item \kw{The Hallways:} Deal the remaining 48 cards in rows \emph{across} the eight \kw{hallways} from
			left to right—one row face down, the next face up—repeating until each column holds six cards (three
			under \kw{fog}, ending with a face-up \kw{mouth} card).
		\end{enumerate}
		
		\rh{The Boss, Generals \& Monsters}
		The first King \emph{face up} in a hallway is the \kw{Boss}—turn it \kw{90 degrees} (sideways) to
		mark it. The Boss's suit determines the primary \kw{Monster Type} infesting that tomb and which
		\kw{affliction} (see chart above) is active. A King hidden under fog is not the Boss until uncovered,
		and a King dealt onto your Hero is simply a Hero card—so a crawl can begin with no active Boss. The
		remaining three Kings are \kw{Generals}. Unlike generic Rats, every \kw{Monster} (Ace--Queen of the
		Boss's suit) and all four Kings (the Boss and its Generals) inflict a negative affliction.
		
		\columnbreak
		\rh{The Boss's Special Protection}
		\kw{Invulnerable:} You cannot slay the Boss until at least one General is in its grave.
		\kw{Indomitable:} Outside of being slain, the Boss cannot be moved by hand (to a pocket or built on top
		of another card). Only a movement Glyph can lift it from a hallway, and only \kw{CYDSTEPP} can set it in
		an empty hallway.
		
		\rh{Status Track: health, combat \& Hero slots}
		Your Status Track acts as both your FreeCells and your life bar:
		\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=8pt,itemsep=0.15pt,topsep=0.3pt,parsep=0pt,label=\suit{$\bullet$}]
			\item \kw{Hearts (HP):} Every \emph{face-up} card in the track is 1 Heart (a two-card PISORF stack counts
			as 2 Hearts). Empty pockets are open storage, not Hearts.
			\item \kw{Level \& Combat Wounds:} Your \kw{Level} is \textbf{1 + the lowest grave's top rank},
			capped at \textbf{Level 12} (an empty grave is 0, so you start at Level 1; no level-ups occur after all
			Jacks are slain). Slaying a card applies wounds equal to \textbf{Card Rank minus Your Level} (minimum
			0)—you must have \textbf{at least 1 Heart left} afterward, or the slay is illegal.
			\item \kw{Taking Wounds \& Healing:} Each wound flips the \emph{rightmost} face-up track card face
			down, working inward (bottom card first in a PISORF stack). Face-down cards are \kw{locked}—they cannot
			be moved, slain, or used. Healing flips wounded cards face up from the \emph{leftmost} wounded card
			outward.
			\item \kw{Leveling Up \& Abilities:} Kin and Class traits stay active while face up. Glyphs and Items
			turn sideways (\kw{exhausted}) when used (Items stay spent; exhausted cards still count as Hearts and
			can leave their pocket). Whenever your Level increases, \kw{heal 1 wound} (2 with a Priest) and turn
			all exhausted Glyphs upright (\kw{refreshed}).
			\item \kw{Converting (Hero Chip):} A Hero aspect leaves play only by conversion, and only through the
			chip: click the chip and the face-up aspect immediately to its \emph{left} is spent for
			good---its ability is gone. The card stays lying in its pocket as plain storage (free to be
			moved, stashed or sent home like any other card), the chip steps one space left, and you
			\kw{heal 1 wound}. A wounded aspect cannot be converted, and the chip never steps back.
		\end{itemize}
		
		\rh{Turn actions}
		On your turn, perform any legal action in any order:
		\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=8pt,itemsep=0.15pt,topsep=0.3pt,parsep=0pt,label=\suit{$\bullet$}]
			\item \kw{Move (Build):} Place an exposed hallway \kw{mouth} card—or a valid descending,
			alternating-color \kw{run} starting at a mouth—onto a mouth card of the opposite color and one rank
			higher. A run may contain at most \textbf{empty pockets + 1} cards. Face-up pocket cards may move to a
			valid hallway mouth, another empty pocket, or a grave.
			\item \kw{Stash (Free Cell):} Move an exposed mouth card into an empty pocket. Stashed Boss's suits and
			Kings trigger their affliction.
			\item \kw{Dig:} When a hallway's mouth card is face down (fog), flip it face up and \kw{heal 1 wound}
			(2 with a Priest). Nothing flips fog except digging (or Venom/Purity). You may leave fogged cards un-dug
			until you need the heal.
			\item \kw{Use Glyph / Item:} Turn a ready Glyph or Item sideways (\kw{exhausted}) to apply its effect—either
			on its own (e.g., WEYTWUT, Potion) or as part of a move or slay (e.g., BYSSEPS, Remedy).
			\item \kw{Slay (Foundation Play):} Move an exposed mouth card or face-up pocket card onto its suit's
			\kw{grave} (built Ace to King). You may play a card one rank above the grave's top card—or lower, slid
			underneath, keeping skipped ranks playable later. Ranks your own Hero cards hold count as home, so a
			drafted 3 and 4 let that suit's grave take a 5 straight off the 2. Slaying is the only way cards leave
			play.
			\item \kw{Convert:} Click the Hero Chip to spend the aspect beside it: its pocket becomes plain
			storage, it stays there as an ordinary card, the chip steps one space left and you heal 1 wound. A
			wounded aspect locks this, as does a wounded neighbour.
			\item \kw{Action Order:} When multiple effects trigger together, resolve in order: \textbf{(1)} exhaust
